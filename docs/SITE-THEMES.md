# The Family of Sites
### Themes proposed, tested, and what each must not become

**Started 2026-08-17** from the user's proposals: *"Equivalence Theme could be a Kitchen or Baking. Mr Fraction Baking Competition. Mr Fraction Factor Mining. Mr Fraction Interior Designer for Area."*

Each is tested against [`MR-FRACTION-PHILOSOPHY.md`](MR-FRACTION-PHILOSOPHY.md) §2.1 — **(a)** name the central noun and the mathematical object it *is*, with no "represents"; **(b)** name the verb, and check it is something the student does *to* the mathematics. Concepts and their difficulty come from [`STRUCTURE-MAP.md`](STRUCTURE-MAP.md).

---

## 0. The family, checked as a system first

The single largest risk in a family of themed sites is not that a theme is weak. It is that **two sites end up doing the same thing in different costumes**, which is invisible while you are inside either one.

| site | topic | verb | status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Factory** | fractions as quantities | **ASSEMBLE** | built |
| **The Word Problem Express** | additive & multiplicative schemas | **NAVIGATE** | built |
| **Factor Mining** | factoring, numbers *and* binomials | **EXTRACT** | next |
| **The Kitchen** | equivalence | **TRANSFORM** | proposed |
| **Interior Design** | area, and space divided | **DECOMPOSE** | proposed |

**Five sites, five distinct verbs.** The family is coherent, and each theme's central risk (below) is the risk of collapsing into a neighbour's verb.

**Worth noticing:** these three proposals land on clusters **A** and **D** of `STRUCTURE-MAP.md` §2 — the largest unbuilt cluster and the fourth — plus the Mine already argued. The themes were proposed before that ranking existed in any conversation, which is mild evidence the instinct and the framework are tracking the same thing.

**Verbs still unused, if the family grows:** RELATE (functions — the wall where most students stop), COMPARE (the referent; directed number), GENERALISE (patterns, exponent rules derived rather than memorised, proof).

## 1. The Kitchen — equivalence

### It passes, and better than the others

**(a) The noun.** A **measure IS a quantity's clothing.** `1/2 cup`, `2/4 cup`, `8 tablespoons` — one amount of flour, three descriptions. The flour is the object; the vessel is the notation. No "represents" needed.

**(b) The verb.** **TRANSFORM** — the student re-measures, substitutes, scales. Distinct from ASSEMBLE.

### Why this is the strongest theme of the three

`STRUCTURE-MAP.md` §2A says the picture for equivalence must show **an object visibly conserving while its presentation changes** — one thing being re-dressed, not two things with an equals sign between them.

**A kitchen does that physically.** Pour flour from a 1/2-cup measure into two 1/4-cup measures and *nothing was added or removed.* The conservation is not illustrated, it is **enacted** — the student watches the same stuff move between descriptions.

> **And that is not a coincidence.** Pouring liquid between differently-shaped vessels is the classic developmental test for conservation of quantity — the canonical demonstration that a learner can or cannot hold "amount" separate from "appearance." **The theme borrows an instrument that is already known to isolate exactly the cognition in question.** For a concept whose failure mode is *"rewriting creates a new object"*, there may be no better physical analogue available.

It also hands you the equals sign for free. `1/2 cup = 8 tbsp` is not an instruction to compute; it is a statement that two labels name one amount. **That is the relational reading of `=`**, which `STRUCTURE-MAP.md` §1.1 identifies as the root misconception in school algebra — taught by a measuring cup rather than by a lecture.

And it comes with built-in verification: **substitute wrongly and the cake fails.** That is self-monitoring made physical, and it is a better check than the Express's estimate gate because it is exact rather than approximate.

### Two risks, and the second is the serious one

**Risk 1 — it collapses into the Factory.** Baking is combining ingredients to make a thing, which is ASSEMBLE, which is already taken. **The discipline: this site is about MEASURING, not about MAKING.** The scene is not "bake a cake." It is *"you are out of the 1/3 cup — what else gets you there?"* Substitution and re-measurement are the whole subject; the cake is why you care. If a screen is ever about combining rather than re-describing, it belongs to the Factory.

**Risk 2 — "Competition" collides with a non-negotiable, and with the audience.**

`PEDAGOGY.md` non-negotiable 7: **no scores, no percentages, no letter grades — progress is described, not graded.** Non-negotiable 11: help is never remedial. And `PEDAGOGY.md` §0 defines the audience as students with **low mathematical self-concept who read confusion as evidence they "aren't a math person."**

**A competition ranks people.** However warm the tent, the format's engine is comparison against others and elimination. That is the one dynamic this project has spent every review cycle refusing to build. It would also be a strange thing to hold alongside a trip report that opens *"No score, no percentage. This is about how you travelled."*

**But the appeal is real and worth keeping** — a competition supplies stakes, warmth, a reason to care, and a natural episode structure. So keep the engine and drop the ranking:

| keep | drop |
|---|---|
| a **brief** — *"tonight: halve this recipe with the wrong measures"* | placings, scores, elimination |
| a **judgement against the recipe** — did it rise? | judgement against other bakers |
| a **kitchen you return to**, with episodes | a leaderboard |

**The one-word fix:** *Mr Fraction's* **Test Kitchen** — where a test kitchen's whole purpose is trying a substitution to see whether it holds. It keeps every warm thing about the baking frame, discards the ranking, and describes the mathematics more accurately than "competition" does. **Your call, and worth making deliberately rather than by default** — if you want the competition, it should be a decision recorded against non-negotiable 7, the way non-negotiable 4 was amended.

## 2. Factor Mining — factoring

**Settled, and argued in `MR-FRACTION-PHILOSOPHY.md` §6.** Passes (a): **a vein IS a factor** — in the rock before any miner arrived, found rather than created. Passes (b): **EXTRACT**, distinct from every other verb in the family.

**The name is right.** "Factor Mining" says both halves — the object and the act — which is more than "Word Problem Express" manages.

Two things already established and worth restating so they are not re-litigated:

- **One site, two depths.** Factoring 72 and factoring `x² − 5x + 6` are the same act — the same seam worked deeper. Numbers, then expressions.
- **The recognition task exists at both depths:** *is this irreducible, or does it come apart — and along which seam?* Common factor, difference of squares, trinomial are **seam types**.

**Its open design problem is unchanged:** the descent from numbers to binomials must feel like *depth* rather than like a different mine.

## 3. Interior Design — area

### It passes, but the framing you choose decides whether it is worth building

**(a) The noun.** A **floor IS an area**; a **room IS a rectangle**; **laying out a room IS partitioning a quantity.** Passes.

**(b) The verb.** **DECOMPOSE** — the student divides a space and fits things into it.

### The reframe that makes it a real site

**As "compute the area", it is thin.** Area formulas are a narrow topic, mostly procedural, and would not sustain a site — you would end up with stations that gate nothing, which `MR-FRACTION-PHILOSOPHY.md` §4.3 names as the worst available failure.

**As "space, divided", it is one of the richest sites on the list.** `STRUCTURE-MAP.md` §2D:

- **Tiling a floor is the distributive property, physically.** A 12×7 room laid as (10×7) + (2×7) is not an illustration of `12×7 = 10×7 + 2×7` — it *is* that identity, in flooring.
- **Composite rooms** — an L-shape decomposed two different ways giving the same total — is conservation under partition, and a sibling of the Kitchen's conservation under re-measurement.
- **Area vs perimeter**, the classic confusion, is not a definitions problem. It is a **referent** problem (`STRUCTURE-MAP.md` §1.2): *how much floor* versus *how much skirting board*. Two questions about one room, and a picture that keeps them apart does most of the teaching.

> **The rule to hold:** the room's dimensions are what the client gives you. **The area is what the student produces.** Label the sides, leave the floor as the question — which is `MR-FRACTION-PHILOSOPHY.md` §2.2 unchanged, in a new costume.

### And this is where the family stops being a set and becomes a sequence

**The area model is the Mine's key diagram for binomials.** Factoring `x² + 5x + 6` with an area model means: *here is the floor, what are its sides?* — the exact inverse of Interior Design's *here are the sides, what is the floor?*

**So Interior Design teaches the Mine's prerequisite, and the Mine runs it backwards.** That is a genuine curricular relationship between two sites, and it is the first one in this family. It suggests something worth deciding deliberately: **do these sites form a set, or a progression?** A shared diagram used forwards on one site and backwards on another is a strong argument for the second, and it would give a student a reason to visit them in an order.

### Minor note on the name

*"Interior Designer"* leans decorative, and design is a matter of taste in a way mathematics is not — a small mismatch with a site whose subject is constraint. **A renovation or floor-laying frame** (coverage, fit, how much do I need) puts the constraint in the foreground and keeps the same warmth. Not a problem, just a nudge — and the honest counterweight is that "Interior Designer" is a *job*, which makes it a place a person works, and that has served this family well twice already.

## 4. What would change these verdicts

- **The Kitchen turning out to be about combining** rather than re-measuring. That is the Factory, and two sites with one verb is the failure §0 exists to catch.
- **Interior Design shipping area formulas** rather than partition. Thin topic, gated nothing, furniture choosing the mathematics.
- **A student who cannot say what a vein is** after riding the Mine — `MR-FRACTION-PHILOSOPHY.md` §4.1's test, and the one that would show a metaphor is decoration.
- **Teachers asking for the competition to rank**, which would be non-negotiable 7 meeting the real world. That is a decision for the user, recorded, not a thing to concede quietly.
